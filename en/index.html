About me

I’m a PhD student at The University of Hong Kong studying how characters interact with players. At miHoYo, I contributed to in-house language model pretraining, post-training for NPC question answering in Honkai: Star Rail, and the multiplayer barista in Petit Planet.

A question I kept coming back to while working on the barista was how she could tell when someone in a group was speaking to her. I’m also interested in what a character should remember from earlier encounters and how it can infer what changed while a player was away.

When should the barista speak?

At the café in Petit Planet, a player might ask a friend for coffee or place an order with the barista. The words can be identical in both exchanges. What should the character do?

Research concept

Tidal Wetland

A player opens a sluice gate and returns later to find a footpath underwater. To plan a route, the character needs to distinguish the effect of the open gate from the incoming tide. This fictional wetland also offers a setting for tracking shifts in habitat and animal activity.

Research concept

The Repair Station

A character hands a spare part to the player. When should their hands meet, the item change ownership, and the quest register the handover? I want to use this small interaction to study how animation and game state can stay in sync.

Manuscripts in preparation

I am the sole first author of each of the following manuscripts.

  1. Counterfactual Post-Training for Multiplayer Game NPCs

    I study whether post-training on paired interactions can teach an NPC when to listen, clarify, or act when only the addressee or the owner of an item changes.

  2. Action-Conditioned World Models for Persistent Game Ecosystems

    I study how to distinguish environmental change from the effects of a player’s actions under partial observation, so that an agent can predict which routes and resources remain available.

  3. Task-Conditioned Visual Memory for Game Agents under Fixed Token Budgets

    An agent retracing its steps may need to remember a turn in the path or a tool left on a bench. I study whether task-conditioned selection preserves the right visual evidence under a fixed token budget.
